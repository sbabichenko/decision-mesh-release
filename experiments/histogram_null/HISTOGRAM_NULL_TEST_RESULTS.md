# Deterministic pool-effect histogram null experiment

Date: 2026-07-28

## Purpose

Test the proposed non-Monte-Carlo gate:

1. estimate or supply a size-stratified pool-effect law;
2. evaluate each exact hierarchical candidate against that law;
3. convert the candidate-specific null tail to a two-sided p-value;
4. apply BH directly to the candidate p-values;
5. leave the accepted B1 exact stage solver and exact commit path unchanged.

No random null simulation or permutation is used.

## Implemented variants

### A. Deterministic score-tail null

For each candidate, its full hierarchical column is evaluated on the current exact-fit state.  Each
pool's binomial score contribution is integrated over a two-component Gaussian approximation to
the supplied pool-effect histogram using deterministic Gauss-Hermite quadrature.  The largest
mixture-sensitive contributors are retained explicitly and the remainder is moment-collapsed.  This
produces a candidatewise two-sided p-value and Gaussian-equivalent signed score.

Three laws were tried:

- Gaussian pool effect with variance 0.35;
- measured mixture shape with total variance fixed at 0.35;
- the full measured size-stratified law in `auditing/law_shared.json`.

For v4, the shipped per-pool `sigma2_pool` column overrides the stratum total variance.

### B. Marginal-mixture score

A second deterministic variant integrated the pool effect directly out of each pool's exact binomial
likelihood using mode-centered nine-node Gauss-Hermite quadrature.  The candidate statistic was the
marginal-likelihood score divided by its observed information, followed by candidatewise BH.

## Correctness fix found during the experiment

The first candidatewise-BH implementation encoded BH discoveries as pseudo-lfdr values 0/1 and then
ran the mean-lfdr prefix a second time.  This appended approximately `q/(1-q)` arbitrary failed
hypotheses after the BH discoveries.  The branch now uses the BH rejection count directly.  All
results below use the corrected implementation.

## Certified-null results

The standing metric counts active faces with area-depth at least 8 and centroid in
WALA [105,175], WAC [3.5,5.5].  Target: v3 approximately 2; v4 between 3 and 7.

| Gate/null construction | v3 false deep faces | v4 false deep faces | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| B3 sandwich reference | 80 | did not close | rejected previously |
| Histogram score, Gaussian variance 0.35 | 80 | not run | rejected |
| Histogram score, measured shape / total variance 0.35 | 40 | 78 | rejected |
| Histogram score, full measured stratum law | 35 | not applicable as the v4 point variances override totals | rejected |
| Marginal-mixture score, Gaussian variance 0.35 | 311 | not run | strongly rejected |

The histogram shape is directionally useful—it reduced v3 from 80 to 40, and the larger measured
stratum variances reduced it to 35—but it does not restore the declared certification scale.

## Real-data regression

The full measured histogram law was also run on the original real-data split.

- held-out marginal NLL: **2.9620**;
- WALA 0--2 fitted boundary: **-5.68** against the law-marginal reference **-7.95**.

Both fail the standing pins (NLL no worse than about 2.9447; boundary at or below -6.9).  The law is
therefore too conservative to use as the production gate in this form.

## Binding diagnostic: the old face count is no longer a candidate-FDR metric

The most important result is structural.

Under the full-law v3 run:

- certified face count: 35;
- gate-admitted vertices centered inside the certified window: 14, all at hierarchical depths 4--5;
- gate-admitted vertices at hierarchical depth at least 8 inside the window: **0**;
- the 35 counted faces have area-depths 8--10, but their maximum vertex hierarchical depth is only
  4 or 5.

Under the v4 histogram run:

- certified face count: 78;
- gate-admitted vertices at hierarchical depth at least 8 inside the window: **0**;
- counted faces have area-depths 8--12, while their maximum vertex hierarchical depth is only 4--6.

Thus the reported "false deep faces" are not deep statistical discoveries under the B1 semantics.
They are geometric completion generated by shallower admitted splits and newest-vertex-bisection
conformity cascades.  B1 deliberately separated geometric existence from statistical freedom; after
that separation, the legacy face-count instrument no longer measures the FDR of the candidate
hypotheses being tested.

This also explains why increasingly accurate candidate nulls do not drive the face count to 2: the
remaining count is largely a geometry-allocation quantity, not a tail-calibration quantity.

## Conclusion

The deterministic histogram-null idea is **not accepted as the B3 replacement in its present form**.
It prices kurtosis and candidate heterogeneity without Monte Carlo, but:

1. the score-tail version remains far above the v3/v4 face pins;
2. the full measured law sacrifices both boundary recovery and held-out NLL;
3. the marginal-mixture score over-refines badly;
4. the standing face-count metric is misaligned with the post-B1 distinction between conformity
   geometry and free hierarchical coefficients.

## Recommended next decision

Before further calibration, choose the inferential unit explicitly.

- **Candidate FDR:** count gate-admitted free hierarchical coefficients whose true surplus is null;
  conformity-only vertices and faces are not discoveries.
- **Geometry budget:** separately measure the number of faces/cascade activations created by each
  admitted split.  If the old face pin must remain an acceptance criterion, use a closure-cost-aware
  allocation rule (for example, candidate p-values plus a precomputed NVB closure cost), and describe
  it as geometric resource control rather than null calibration.

The immediate engineering experiment should be per-admission cascade-cost logging and a frozen
candidate-level null ledger.  It should not be another global scale or tail-threshold adjustment.
